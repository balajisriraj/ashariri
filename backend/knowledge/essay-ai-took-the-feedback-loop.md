---
title: "AI Didn't Take the Coding. It Took the Feedback Loop."
url: "https://balajisriraj.com/writing/ai-took-the-feedback-loop"

---

Programming used to pay out five times an hour. Understand the problem, get the
syntax working, hit a wall and find something close, watch it compile. Agents
collect most of those small rewards now, and what is left is a single payout at
the end: does the product work.

The argument is that this is not nostalgia. Peter Naur wrote in 1985 that a
program is a theory held in the minds of the people who built it, and that the
source code is only the residue. Hand the whole build to an agent and you get
the residue without the theory. It runs, and nobody can properly change it.

The piece lands on a working altitude: one module per cycle rather than one
feature or one line, and three questions asked before anything merges.
